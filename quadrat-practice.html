Quadrat Practice: reading percent cover

Four 1 m × 1 m quadrats along a schoolyard tape · record every row · your column must total 100%
Remind me how to do this
  1. Look straight down. Percent cover is what hides the ground when you look from directly above — not how many plants there are.
  2. Use the grid. 10 × 10 = 100 cells, so one cell = 1%. Count cells instead of guessing. Two half-cells make one whole cell.
  3. One species at a time. Count the patchy ones first.
  4. Don't count the dominant species. Give it whatever is left over — 100 minus everything else. Faster, and no extra error.
  5. Bare ground and leaf litter count. Every bit of ground is covered by something, so the column adds to 100%.
